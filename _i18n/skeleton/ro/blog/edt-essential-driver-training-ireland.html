EDT in Ireland: your 12 sessions, logbook, sponsor and MyEDT

Essential Driver Training in plain English: what the sessions cover, who keeps the official record, what a sponsor does and the steps to your driving test.

If you are learning to drive a car in Ireland, Essential Driver Training (EDT) is the set of lessons you must finish before you can sit your driving test. This guide explains how it works and what to do at each step. Checked on 13 October 2026 against the Road Safety Authority (RSA) pages listed at the end. DTT Ireland is an independent study app, not the RSA.

What EDT is

EDT is 12 one-hour lessons, which the RSA calls sessions, with an Approved Driving Instructor (ADI). Only an ADI can give EDT sessions. You must do EDT if your first car learner permit was issued on or after 4 April 2011; the RSA says these permits show code 991.

Each session has its own topic. You take session 1 first. Sessions 2 to 8 can be taken in any order, and sessions 9 to 12 in any order once 2 to 8 are done. There is no exam at the end: your ADI gives you spoken and written feedback after every session.

Reduced EDT for some foreign licence holders

If you hold a full car licence from a country that has no licence exchange agreement with Ireland, you may qualify for reduced EDT: six sessions instead of 12 (sessions 1, 5, 6, 7, 9 and 10), and no six-month wait before your driving test. The RSA’s conditions include holding an Irish learner permit, having held your foreign licence for at least two years before you got that permit, your foreign licence not having expired by more than six months when you apply, and living in Ireland.

You apply by post to the National Driver Licence Service (NDLS), which writes to you with the outcome. If you are approved, show the letter to your ADI. If your licence is from a country that does have an exchange agreement with Ireland, you may be able to exchange it instead, without EDT or a driving test. The form and the full conditions are on the RSA’s reduced EDT page.

Your logbook and MyEDT: the official record

When you start, your ADI gives you an EDT logbook. After each session, the ADI signs, stamps and dates the logbook and uploads the session to MyEDT, the RSA’s online record of your training. The logbook also has a page for your own notes on what to practise and what to ask your ADI next time.

In MyEDT you can see the sessions you have done, when each was uploaded and which are left, and you can print a record. You sign in with the details on your learner permit card. The RSA says to allow at least 10 working days for a new session to appear; after that, contact your ADI. If a date or session is wrong, ask the ADI who gave that session to correct it.

Two habits help. Ask at the start of each lesson whether it counts as an EDT session. And keep your logbook safe: if you lose it, the RSA has a lost logbook process and your ADI can give you a new book.

We are not the RSA, and we don’t keep your EDT record.

Only your ADI’s entries in your logbook and in MyEDT count. DTT Ireland, our Android app and our web app do not record EDT, and we don’t offer an EDT tracker. Check your progress in MyEDT.

The sponsor: optional but useful

A sponsor is an experienced driver who comes with you on practice drives and helps you improve. The RSA says your sponsor must have held a full driving licence for at least two years. It can be a family member or friend who meets this, or your ADI. Ideally, your sponsor talks to your ADI about your progress. Your logbook explains who can be a sponsor.

You don’t have to have a sponsor, and you can change sponsor. But whenever you drive on a learner permit, you must be accompanied by an ADI or by someone who has held a full licence for the same category for more than two years, and a sponsor can be that person. The other learner permit rules still apply: L-plates, insurance, tax and NCT, and no motorways.

Practice between sessions

Some countries ask learners to log a set number of supervised driving hours. The RSA’s EDT pages set no such number for car learners in Ireland: the record that counts is your EDT sessions. Practice still matters, and the RSA recommends:

  • at least three hours of practice on what your last session covered, with your sponsor or your instructor;
  • about two weeks between sessions, so that EDT is spread over around six months;
  • extra lessons alongside EDT, and reading the EDT booklet and the matching part of the Rules of the Road before each session.

EDT is only part of your training. When you finish it, keep practising and take more lessons if you need them, until your ADI says you are ready for the test.

When you can book your driving test

You can apply for your driving test before you finish EDT. But you can’t book a test appointment until all of these are true:

  • you have held your first learner permit for at least six months (the six-month rule);
  • you have completed all 12 EDT sessions;
  • all 12 have been showing in your MyEDT account for at least one day.

With reduced EDT the six-month rule doesn’t apply, but your six sessions must be showing in MyEDT for at least a day. The RSA’s driving test eligibility checker takes you through it.

Your EDT, step by step

  1. Get your first learner permit. You need to pass the theory test first. EDT starts once you have the permit.
  2. Choose an ADI. Use the RSA’s register and pick one who teaches category B (car). There is no standard fee, so compare a few. Paying for many sessions in advance makes it harder to switch.
  3. Start with session 1. Your ADI gives you the logbook. Ask whether each lesson counts as EDT.
  4. Check each entry. After every session, make sure the logbook is signed, stamped and dated.
  5. Practise in between. Aim for at least three hours on what you just covered, with your sponsor or ADI, and leave about two weeks before the next session.
  6. Check MyEDT. A session can take 10 working days to appear. Ask your ADI to fix anything missing or wrong.
  7. Book your test when you are eligible. You need six months on your first permit and all sessions showing in MyEDT for at least a day. Book when your ADI agrees you are ready.

Find an instructor on the RSA register of Approved Driving Instructors.

Common questions

Does DTT Ireland record my EDT sessions?

No. Only your ADI records EDT, in your logbook and in MyEDT. Our apps help you study for the theory test and keep no EDT record.

Do I have to do all 12 sessions with the same ADI?

No. Once your ADI has signed, stamped and dated your logbook and uploaded the session, you can move to another ADI for the next one.

Is there a test at the end of EDT?

No. Each session has set content and your ADI gives you feedback, but there is no exam at the end.

If I fail the driving test, do I have to repeat EDT?

No, failing the test doesn’t mean repeating EDT. If your learner permit has lapsed for a long time, check the RSA’s FAQs, because you may have to start again.

Does my sponsor have to be a driving instructor?

No. A responsible driver who meets the RSA’s criteria can be your sponsor, and your ADI can be your sponsor too.

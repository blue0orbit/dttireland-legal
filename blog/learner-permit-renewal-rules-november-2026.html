Ireland’s learner-permit renewal changes: 1 November 2026

What learners should check before their next permit renewal, with the RSA’s announced start date.

An upcoming change

As of 28 September 2026, the RSA says new renewal rules start on 1 November 2026. They affect repeated permits for car, work-vehicle and motorcycle categories.

What changes?

Applying for a test alone will no longer qualify you for a third or fourth permit: you must have taken a test in the preceding two years. The RSA also says there will be no fifth permit; after the fourth, learners must restart the learning process. A permit expired for two years or more will require restarting, across all learner categories.

Existing valid permits do not suddenly stop being valid on the announcement date. First-permit holders can still obtain a second permit without first taking a driving test. Read the RSA’s detailed rules, including medically restricted permits and what restarting involves.

Your planning checklist

  • Write down your permit expiry date and which permit you hold.
  • Check your test history against the official guidance.
  • Check your application status and training requirements now.
  • Leave time for a test invitation; applying is not the same as attending.

The RSA application guide explains the invitation process. Keep your contact details up to date and check your account rather than relying on an estimated date.

Another November change: theory-test minimum age

The official theory-test service says a minimum age of 15 will apply to test appointments from 1 November 2026. Younger learners and anyone adding a licence category should read its current age and certificate-validity FAQs before booking.

Make practice purposeful

Keep theory revision separate from practical preparation. Review mistakes in small sessions, then discuss recurring road situations with your instructor. An app can help you revise; it cannot replace required training or make you eligible for a test.

DTT Ireland is an independent study app, not the RSA, NDLS or the official test booking service. Check official guidance before booking or relying on a rule.

Learner rules

L plates, N plates and penalty points: rules for learner and novice drivers in Ireland

What the law, the RSA and An Garda Síochána say about plates, tabards, accompanying drivers, motorways, penalty points and alcohol while you learn and in your first two years.

In Ireland, learner drivers must display L plates, and learner riders wear an L tabard. Car learners must always have a qualified driver with them, and learners can’t use motorways. After you pass the driving test, you are a novice driver for two years and must display N plates. Learner and novice drivers are both disqualified at 7 penalty points instead of 12, and both have a lower drink-drive limit. This guide sets out each rule and the penalties, checked on 14 October 2026 against the RSA, An Garda Síochána and the legislation listed at the end. DTT Ireland is an independent study app, not the RSA.

L plates and L tabards

If you drive a car, van, truck or bus on a learner permit, you must display L plates at the front and rear of the vehicle. The law describes them as rectangular plates with a red letter L, at least 15 cm high, on a white background, in a clearly visible upright position. An Garda Síochána reminds learners that the plates must be on any vehicle they drive, at all times, and that includes EDT lessons with an instructor.

Learner motorcyclists and moped riders don’t use plates. Instead they wear a yellow fluorescent tabard over their outer clothing, with the same red L on a white background on the front and the back.

Not displaying L plates or the tabard is a penalty point offence: a €120 fixed charge and 2 penalty points, or 4 points if it goes to court.

Who can accompany a learner driver

In a car, van, truck or bus, a learner must be accompanied at all times by a qualified person who supervises the drive. That is either an Approved Driving Instructor (ADI) or a person who has held a full driving licence for the same category for a continuous two years, without being disqualified in that time. If the car has a manual gearbox, the accompanying driver’s licence must not be limited to automatic cars (code 78).

This rule means a novice driver can’t accompany you, because a novice has held a full licence for less than two years. The main exception is the driving test itself, when you drive with the tester instead.

The accompanying driver is not the same as the sponsor in Essential Driver Training. A sponsor is optional and helps you practise between lessons, and a sponsor who meets the two-year rule can also be your accompanying driver. Our EDT guide explains the sponsor’s role.

Motorcycle learners are not accompanied in the same way. Before riding on a public road they must complete Initial Basic Training (IBT) with an approved instructor and carry the IBT certificate. Our IBT guide covers the course.

Driving unaccompanied: what happens

Driving unaccompanied on a learner permit is an offence. The fixed charge is €160 and 2 penalty points, or 4 points on conviction in court. Since 22 December 2018, under what is known as the Clancy Amendment, Gardaí can also seize the car an unaccompanied learner is driving.

If the car belongs to someone else, it can still be seized, and the owner can be prosecuted for letting an unaccompanied learner drive it, with a fine of up to €1,000. In December 2023, An Garda Síochána said that 4,708 vehicles had been seized from unaccompanied learners since the law changed, and 8,149 fixed charge notices issued for driving unaccompanied.

Parents and car owners: this applies to you too.

Lending your car to a learner who drives it alone can mean losing the car to a seizure and facing a prosecution yourself.

Other limits on a learner permit

On a learner permit you can drive on all public roads except motorways, and only to learn. An Garda Síochána lists these limits:

  • no driving on a motorway, in any vehicle category;
  • no carrying passengers for payment;
  • no towing a trailer on a car, truck or bus learner permit;
  • no passenger on a motorcycle or moped learner permit;
  • on a tractor (category W) learner permit, a passenger only if the vehicle is built to carry one and the passenger has held a driving licence for two years.

You also need to hold a valid learner permit for the vehicle you are driving. If you haven’t got one yet, our guide to getting your first learner permit explains the steps after the theory test.

N plates: your first two years

When you pass the driving test and get your first full driving licence, you become a novice driver for two years from the date the licence is issued. During that time you must display N plates at the front and rear of the vehicle: a red letter N, at least 15 cm high, on a white background. Novice motorcyclists wear a yellow fluorescent tabard with the N on the front and back.

If you are disqualified, or don’t hold a licence, during those two years, that time doesn’t count, so your novice period ends later. Not displaying N plates or the tabard carries the same penalty as the L plate offence: a €120 fixed charge and 2 penalty points, or 4 on conviction.

As a novice you can drive alone and use motorways, but you can’t accompany a learner, and the lower limits on penalty points and alcohol still apply to you.

Penalty points: 7 instead of 12

Most drivers are disqualified for six months when they reach 12 penalty points. If you are a learner or a novice driver when points are added to your record, you are disqualified at 7. Penalty points stay on your record for three years, so points you get as a learner can still count while you are a novice. Every penalty point offence counts towards the total, not only the learner ones.

The lower drink-drive limit

The law calls learner and novice drivers “specified persons”. Their limit is 20 mg of alcohol per 100 ml of blood (9 µg per 100 ml of breath, or 27 mg per 100 ml of urine). For most other drivers the limit is 50 mg per 100 ml of blood (22 µg per 100 ml of breath). The same lower limit applies when you drive a truck or bus, to taxi drivers at work, and to anyone driving without a licence for that vehicle.

For a specified person between the lower limit and 80 mg, the law sets a fixed penalty of €200 and a three-month disqualification. Higher readings go to court, with longer disqualifications. The lower limit leaves very little room, so the safest choice while you are a learner or novice is not to drink at all before you drive.

Common questions

Do I need L plates when my instructor is in the car?

Yes. An Garda Síochána says learners must display L plates at all times on any vehicle they drive, including during EDT lessons with an ADI.

Can someone who passed their test last year sit in with me?

No. The accompanying driver must have held a full licence for the same category for a continuous two years, without disqualification, or be an ADI.

Can a learner drive on a motorway with an instructor?

No. Learner permit holders are not allowed on motorways in any vehicle category, even with an instructor.

When can I take my N plates off?

Two years after your first full driving licence was issued. Any time you were disqualified or had no licence during those two years is added on.

Do penalty points from my learner permit follow me?

Yes. Points stay on your record for three years, and while you are a learner or a novice, 7 points means a six-month disqualification.

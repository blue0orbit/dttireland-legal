Failed your driving theory test in Ireland? How to prepare for a retake

Find out what happens after a failed Irish driving theory test, when to rebook, what feedback you receive and how to prepare for another attempt.

If you fail the Irish Driver Theory Test, you can book another attempt. The test provider strongly advises leaving at least three complete business days between attempts so you can revise. Use the result feedback to choose what to study, then practise with a fresh mock before you go back.

What happens if you fail the Irish theory test?

Your result appears at the end of the test. Read the feedback you receive and write down the areas to revisit. The provider explains that it does not tell you which individual questions you answered incorrectly. Use any topic feedback to guide your review and revise the rest of the syllabus as well. A second test can ask a different set of questions.

For a car (BW) or motorcycle (AM) test, the official booking page lists the current test format and pass mark. For example, scoring 34 in a test that requires 35 out of 40 means you were one mark short, but that number alone does not tell you which rule to review. Use the topics and the mistakes from your own practice to make that decision.

How soon can you rebook your theory test?

TheoryTest.ie’s official FAQ strongly advises waiting at least three complete business days before sitting the test again. Treat that as revision advice from the provider and check the appointment calendar for current availability. Book the correct test category and confirm the current fee on the official booking page. The provider says there is no limit on how many times you may sit the test.

Use the official booking service. Match the name on your booking to the identification you will bring, check the accepted ID list and keep the confirmation. If you need an accommodation, ask the provider before choosing an appointment so there is time to arrange it.

A three-session revision plan

  1. Find the gap. Start with the areas from your result feedback. For each, do a small practice set. Write a short note beside each error: missing rule, confused sign or missed detail.
  2. Revisit the rule. Use the official study material to check the explanation. If two signs look similar, write down what distinguishes them. If you missed a junction question, identify who is moving and which sign or marking governs the situation.
  3. Test the change. Take a fresh mixed mock without pausing to look up answers. Review correct guesses alongside wrong answers. If you still rely on guesses in one topic, return to that topic before your next full mock.

Use the guide to difficult Irish theory-test topics to choose practice areas, or practise in the DTT Ireland web app. Aim for a score above the pass mark across several different sets. A practice result is a study signal, and your official result comes from the test provider.

On your second test day

Check the appointment location and time, pack your original accepted ID and follow the provider’s arrival instructions. Use the practice screen at the centre to get comfortable with the controls if offered. Read the whole question before deciding, and use the review feature if you want to return to an uncertain answer within the allotted time.

If you think a problem with the test itself affected the result, check the official appeals instructions promptly. The provider explains the deadline and how to contact it. If you pass, the first learner permit guide explains the next step.

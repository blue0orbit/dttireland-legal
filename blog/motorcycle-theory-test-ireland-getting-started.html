Motorcycle theory test in Ireland: a practical study plan

Choose the correct theory test, build a motorcycle revision routine and understand the next steps towards your learner permit and IBT.

Car-driving experience is useful background, but it is not a motorcycle study plan. A rider has different equipment, controls and exposure to road conditions. Start with the correct test category, then build revision around understanding the situations behind each answer. Here is a practical route from your first study session to your next official booking.

Choose the correct test before you book

The official theory-test service identifies AM as the motorcycle/moped theory test, covering learner-permit categories A, A1, A2 and AM. BW is the car, tractor and work-vehicle test. AM and BW currently have 40 questions, 45 minutes and a pass mark of 35. Source: official theory-test FAQs.

The exam name and your intended licence category are related but not identical. Before paying, check the RSA route for your chosen vehicle. If you already hold another licence, tell the official service your exact situation rather than assuming that a previous pass removes a requirement.

Build a topic map, not just a question counter

Organise your revision into a few manageable groups. These are suggested study themes, not a claim about the official number of questions in each topic.

A useful way to organise motorcycle revision
ThemeQuestion to ask yourself
Equipment and checksCan I explain the purpose of the item or check?
Observation and positioningWhat information would a rider need before acting?
Road conditionsWhat changes in this situation, and why does it matter?
Other road usersWhose movement might be difficult to predict?
Signs and rulesAm I recognising the meaning or only the picture?

When you miss an answer, add the underlying idea to your notes. “I confused the two signs” is more useful than “question 47 wrong.” It gives your next session a clear purpose even if the app presents a different question.

A flexible seven-session revision plan

Use these as seven study sessions, not a promise that everyone will be ready in one week. Repeat a session when needed and allow more time for unfamiliar material.

  1. Set a baseline. Try a short mixed set without looking up answers. Record uncertain guesses as well as mistakes.
  2. Start with equipment. Read explanations and write three points you could explain to someone else.
  3. Work on observation. Slow down on scenarios and identify the information the question gives you.
  4. Review signs and wording. Separate similar-looking signs and terms in your notes.
  5. Return to earlier errors. Check whether you understand them after a break, rather than immediately after seeing the answer.
  6. Try timed mixed practice. Notice whether time pressure changes your reading or causes rushed guesses.
  7. Review the pattern. Decide which topics need another cycle before booking or sitting the exam.

A useful routine can be short: a few minutes on old mistakes, a focused topic set and a final note about what still needs work. Consistency is more useful than exhausting yourself with a long session you will not repeat.

Use mistakes without memorising answer positions

After choosing an answer, explain why it fits the question and why the alternatives do not. Avoid shortcuts such as remembering that the answer is usually the second button. Pay attention to words that change the scenario, such as weather, visibility and the type of road user.

If an explanation remains unclear, keep it on a separate questions-for-my-instructor list. Do not turn uncertainty into a rule you later apply on the road. A practice app helps you organise learning; it is not a substitute for individual instruction.

Using Motorcycle mode in DTT Ireland

In the latest Android release, choose Motorcycle beside Car on the home screen. Use the dedicated question bank for rider revision. The app includes Simplified Chinese among its nine language choices; see the Chinese study guide for the distinction between app language and official test support.

Check the included features and current price in the app when selecting Pro Car, Pro Motorcycle or Pro Both. Existing Car access does not automatically include Motorcycle. If the new selector is missing, check Google Play for the latest available update before changing settings or buying again.

Booking and what comes after the theory test

Use the official booking service and read the identification and assistance instructions for your appointment. Keep the confirmation in an easily accessible place and compare the appointment date and location with your own calendar.

After a pass, follow the RSA learner-permit application steps. Your next training questions belong with an approved IBT instructor. Our complete IBT guide explains the course routes, a quote-comparison checklist and what to ask before your first day.

Keep the theory booking, permit application, IBT booking and practical-test application as separate entries in your plan. Completing one does not automatically arrange the next.

Common questions

Which theory test is for motorcycles?

The official service labels the motorcycle/moped theory test AM. Check the licence category you want and the official booking selection before paying.

How many questions must I get right?

The official AM/BW format is 40 questions in 45 minutes, with a pass mark of 35. Check the official FAQs for the current format.

Can I use only car questions to prepare?

Use motorcycle-specific material as well. Familiar road rules do not cover every rider-specific topic.

Does passing a mock test guarantee a real pass?

No. Use practice results to identify areas to review. Repeatedly recognising the same questions can overstate your understanding.
